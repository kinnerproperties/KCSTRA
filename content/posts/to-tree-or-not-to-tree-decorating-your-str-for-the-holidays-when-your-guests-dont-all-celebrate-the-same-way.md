# Is it even a question?  (Yes. Or, at least, it should be.)

It's mid-November. Somewhere in a garage, a host is staring down a seven-foot artificial tree, still in a box held together by packing tape and optimism. The tree stares back. Neither one blinks. "Do I really want to do this?" One may ask themself.

The question shows up right on schedule every year, somewhere between the last pumpkin spice latte and the first holiday song in the grocery store: does the tree go up at the rental this year, or does it stay in the box?

No rulebook here, and this post isn't going to write one. Think of it as a few things to chew on over your eggnog. No judgement here.

## **Meet your December guests**

Picture your booking calendar for the month. There's the family of nine who drove six hours for the full storybook Christmas and would be quietly heartbroken without a tree. There's the traveling nurse on a 13-week contract who wants blackout curtains, strong Wi-Fi, and absolutely no jingle bells at 7 a.m. after their night shift.

There's the couple lighting Hanukkah candles in your kitchen. There's the solo traveler who booked specifically to escape the family group chat. And there's the group from overseas who are honestly just curious what all the fuss is about.

Sometimes they're all on your calendar in the same month. Sometimes the same week. So here's a fun exercise before you open that box: who actually stayed with you last December?

## **What a tree says (and what it might not)**

To one guest, a twinkling tree in the corner says, "Welcome. We thought of you." To another, the same tree says, "You're a visitor on someone else's holiday." Same tree. Same lights. Completely different message. Both of them are real, and neither guest is wrong.

Then there's the practical side. The pine needles that turn up in the vacuum well into March. The toddler-versus-glass-ornament matchup - and the ornament never wins. And that one strand of lights that works only when it feels like it.

## **Questions worth sitting with**

Before the box comes out of the garage, a few questions to try on:

- **Who is this for?** The guest, the listing photos, or (be honest) the part of you that just loves decorating?
- **How would it feel to walk in?** Would a guest who doesn't celebrate the same way you do feel welcome, or like they wandered into a stranger's living room?
- **Does "cozy winter" do the same job as "Christmas"?** More on that in a moment.
- **What if guests got to choose?** A tree that's ready to go up on request is a different gift than one that's already there.
- **Did anyone mention it?** Guests who know what to expect before they arrive are rarely surprised, in either direction.

## **My case for "Cozy Winter"**

Here's the good news for those of us who live for decorating but want to be conscious: you don't have to give it up! There's a way to be generous with the season without making it personal.

The idea is simple. Leave the pieces that belong to your own story at home (the nativity, the angels, Santa and his entourage) and lean into what nearly everyone recognizes as winter: cold and snowy outside, warm and golden inside. Think evergreen boughs and pinecones, a basket of birch logs, dried orange slices and cinnamon sticks, plaid and chunky-knit throws, frosted glass, white lights, and plenty of (LED) candlelight. Hang onto a few harvest touches from fall, too. They earn their keep well into January.

It's decorating the old-school way, from before the holidays turned into a neighborhood arms race of inflatables and light shows you can see from space. Again, no judgement here.

There's a lovely thread running through so many winter traditions: LIGHT. Hanukkah candles, Diwali lamps, Lunar New Year lanterns, solstice fires, Advent wreaths. Lanterns, clusters of candles, or a simple star.  Each of them let you borrow that shared warmth as inspiration, without turning anyone's sacred objects into props. A guest from almost any tradition might sense a quiet nod to their own. Everyone else just sees a pretty, warm room.

So what does cozy look like in your space? And could every guest who walks in feel at home in it?

## **Whatever you choose, choose it with purpose**

A full-on wonderland, a blank slate, or something cozy in between: any of them can be the right call for your place and your guests. The only wrong move is deciding on autopilot. Hospitality, at its heart, is about how the guest feels when they walk through the door, regardless of what the host is celebrating.

One gentle thought on safety, because guests don't know your outlets, your breaker box, or which strand runs a little warm. LED candles, lights on timers, a tree base that won't tip when a small hand grabs a branch, and shatterproof ornaments at toddler height go a long way. Call it peace of mind for you and your guests, and a holiday that doesn't include a side trip to urgent care.

So, over to you. Tree, no tree, or tree in a box by the closet door? Tell us in the comments. And if anyone has figured out how to get that tree back into that box and back into the basement in one piece, please share.
