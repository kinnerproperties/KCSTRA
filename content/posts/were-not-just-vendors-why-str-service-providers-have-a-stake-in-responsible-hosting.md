# **We’re Not Just Vendors: Why STR Service Providers Have a Stake in Responsible Hosting**

As the owner of TurnTidy KC, a cleaning company that specializes in vacation rentals, I know that short-term rentals don't operate in a vacuum (pun fully intended).

Behind every successful STR is an entire network of people and businesses: cleaners, property managers, maintenance professionals, photographers, designers, landscapers, real estate agents, bookkeepers, software companies and countless others whose livelihoods benefit from a healthy short-term rental market.

Me and my team work alongside short-term rental hosts throughout the Kansas City metropolitan area. Our role may begin with preparing a property for the next guest, but I've come to believe our responsibility to this industry goes further than providing a great turnover.

**If our businesses benefit from short-term rentals, we also have a stake in protecting the long-term viability of short-term rentals in our community.**

## **The Reputation of STRs Belongs to All of Us**

Most responsible hosts understand that operating a short-term rental means more than putting a property on Airbnb or VRBO and collecting reservation income.

There are licenses to obtain, local ordinances to understand, taxes to address, safety requirements to meet, neighbors to consider and properties to maintain. Those requirements vary across the Kansas City metropolitan area, which makes understanding the rules where a property is actually located especially important.

Unfortunately, the properties that attract the most attention are rarely the ones quietly doing everything right.

They're the ones generating repeated neighborhood complaints. They're poorly maintained. They're operating without required approvals. They're creating parking, trash or noise problems. They're absentee-owned without adequate local oversight.

And when those properties become the public face of short-term rentals, responsible operators pay the price.

Public perception doesn't necessarily distinguish between the host who has invested significant time and money into operating responsibly and the owner who saw an opportunity to make a quick buck.

To a frustrated neighbor, they're both "an Airbnb."

To policymakers responding to complaints, they're both part of the STR industry.

That should matter to every legitimate host—and to every business serving them.

## **A Quick Buck Isn't a Long-Term Business Strategy**

As a business owner, I have no interest in building TurnTidy around an industry that burns brightly for a few years and then becomes so unpopular or heavily restricted that responsible operators can no longer succeed.

I want TurnTidy to be here years from now.

That requires healthy relationships between STR operators, neighborhoods, guests, city governments and the businesses supporting them.

There will always be an opportunity to make money servicing another property. But revenue from a client who is knowingly operating irresponsibly isn't necessarily good revenue.

If the collective result is more neighborhood opposition, more complaints and greater pressure for restrictive regulations, we've traded long-term sustainability for a short-term invoice.

That's not an investment in my business.

## **Service Providers Have More Influence Than We Realize**

Those of us working inside STR properties often see things other people don't.

A cleaner may notice a missing smoke detector before anyone else does. A property manager may recognize that a house regularly exceeds its intended occupancy. A maintenance provider may notice neglected safety issues. A local contact may see recurring trash, parking or neighbor concerns.

That doesn't make us code enforcement officers, attorneys or regulators—and we shouldn't pretend to be.

But it does make us partners in the operation.

For TurnTidy, responsible partnership means being willing to ask questions, communicate concerns and establish standards for the properties and hosts we choose to serve.

It means encouraging clients to understand the licensing and regulatory requirements applicable to their property.

It means taking safety and maintenance concerns seriously rather than assuming, "That's the owner's problem."

And, when necessary, it means being willing to decide that a particular client or property isn't aligned with the way we want to do business.

## **Responsible Hosting Is Bigger Than Compliance**

Legal operation is the baseline. Stewardship goes further.

A successful short-term rental should not only be a positive first impression for the Kansas City Metro but also a good neighbor. 

That means maintaining the property. Managing trash. Addressing noise. Providing guests with clear expectations. Having someone available when problems arise. Respecting the character of the neighborhood. Responding when neighbors raise legitimate concerns.

Those things aren't just good hospitality. They're part of earning the social license that allows STRs to continue operating within our communities.  Every irresponsible property makes that work harder.

Every well-run property makes the case that short-term rentals can coexist successfully with neighborhoods and contribute positively to Kansas City's economy.

## **We Are a Part of the STR Community, Too**

One reason organizations like the Kansas City Short Term Rental Alliance matter is that the conversation shouldn't include only property owners.

The STR economy supports a much larger local ecosystem.

When responsible STRs succeed, local cleaners have work. Maintenance professionals have work. Guests spend money at restaurants, coffee shops and attractions. Local suppliers gain customers. Property owners invest in their homes.

When irresponsible operators damage the industry's reputation, those ripple effects travel in the other direction as well.

That's why I believe businesses serving STR hosts should think of ourselves as more than vendors.

**We are stakeholders.**

We don't need to police our clients, and we certainly don't need to become experts in every municipal ordinance. But we can educate ourselves. We can ask better questions. We can establish professional standards. We can support organizations advocating for responsible hosting. And we can choose not to knowingly participate in practices that threaten the long-term health of the industry supporting our own businesses.

I don't view stewardship as something separate from business.  I view it as part of building a business worth keeping.

Because the goal isn't simply to make money from Kansas City's short-term rental market today.

**It's to help build an STR community that Kansas City will still welcome tomorrow.**
